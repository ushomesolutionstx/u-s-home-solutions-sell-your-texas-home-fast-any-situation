Selling As-Is

What Closing Costs Actually Look Like When You Sell for Cash

The fees are different, the timeline is faster, and your net proceeds are usually higher than you'd expect.

3 min read · U.S. Home Solutions

If you're considering selling your home and wondering whether a cash offer is worth it financially, closing costs are one of the biggest pieces to understand. The traditional selling process stacks on fees that most homeowners don't fully see coming until the settlement statement. A cash sale works differently, and for most sellers, the difference is significant.

What You're Typically Paying in a Traditional Sale

In a traditional home sale, sellers commonly cover 5 to 6 percent in real estate commissions alone. Add lender-required repairs, pre-listing inspections, concessions negotiated after the buyer's inspection, and title fees on both sides, and the actual cost of selling can reach 8 to 10 percent of the sale price. On a $250,000 home, that's $20,000 to $25,000 gone before you see a dollar.

How a Cash Sale Changes the Math

When you sell to a cash buyer, most of those layers come off. There's no listing agent commission on your side. There's no lender involved on the buyer's end, which means no appraisal requirement, no repair requests tied to financing, and no risk of the deal falling through because a loan didn't close. The cash buyer typically covers their own closing fees, and you know from the offer what you'll actually walk away with.

Net proceeds: On a cash sale, sellers commonly keep 97 to 99 percent of the agreed price. On a traditional listing, it's often 91 to 93 percent after all costs are factored in.

The Hidden Cost of Time

One closing cost the traditional process doesn't put on paper is time. Carrying a home through 30, 60, or 90 days of listing, showing, and lender delays means continued mortgage payments, taxes, insurance, and utilities. A cash close can happen in as few as 7 to 14 days. For a homeowner dealing with financial pressure, that timeline difference is its own form of savings.

What to Watch for in a Cash Offer

Not every cash offer is the same. A lower offer from a cash buyer who closes in two weeks may net you more than a higher offer from a financed buyer who takes 60 days, requires repairs, and carries fall-through risk. The number that matters is what lands in your account after everything is paid, not the headline price. Ask for a simple net sheet before you decide. Any honest buyer will provide one.

Selling for cash isn't right for every situation, but if you're weighing your options, understanding the cost difference is the right place to start. A cash offer removes most of the variables that eat into your proceeds and shortens the process considerably. If you have a specific timeline or financial pressure driving your decision, getting a real offer is the fastest way to see the actual numbers.
